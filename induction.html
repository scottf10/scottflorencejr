Exemplar #2 - Pre and Post Assessment of Student Growth in File Systems Navigation


I observed my students ability to navigate a web browser, download and image, and upload it to their Canva project with the goal of getting their knowledge and understanding of filesystems. Students were asked to independently download an image from the internet, locate the file in their Downloads folder, and upload it into their Canva project. Of the 24 students assessed, 13 were able to complete all three steps without assistance. This task was used as a baseline measure of students' file navigation and computer organization skills at the beginning of the instructional period.

 

Students were asked to independently download an image from Google, locate the file in their Downloads folder, and upload it to Google Classroom. Of the 21 students assessed, 19 successfully completed all three steps without assistance. This assessment was used to measure student growth in file navigation and computer organization skills following continued practice throughout the school year.

Looking back at our growth this year, I am incredibly proud of how far my students have come from our baseline Canva assessment, where nearly half the class struggled to navigate the filesystem independently. Thanks to consistent practice and reinforcement throughout the school year, our final Google Classroom assessment showed a massive jump to a 90% success rate, with 19 out of 21 students finishing completely unassisted. This dramatic improvement proves that intentional practice builds true digital confidence, and I will continue providing targeted support to help my remaining students achieve full mastery.

Professional Reflection

This past year has been a period of significant growth and learning as I navigated the complexities of teaching a rapidly evolving computer science curriculum. Balancing established teaching methodologies with modern, practical projects has allowed me to refine my instructional practices, improve classroom management, and strengthen my connections with the broader school community. Below is a comprehensive reflection on my professional journey this year and my goals for the future.

Curriculum, Pacing, and Professional Growth

I have grown a lot this year regarding content knowledge, understanding of the classroom curriculum, and pacing with my semester-long classes. Computer science is a really dynamic field with technologies and platforms constantly changing, so balancing established teaching methodologies and curriculum with practical and modern projects has been challenging and rewarding. Going forward, pacing and accessibility are two areas of focus I have identified to improve. I plan to achieve this by providing video tutorials and additional digital resources for students who fall behind or want to reinforce and expand their knowledge of classroom topics. To support this growth, I work very closely with my department on curriculum development and execution, and I have participated in professional development outside of school hours to expand my knowledge of the current tech and AI environment to keep our curriculum modern and useful. Moving forward, I want to join professional organizations, reach out to more teachers outside of our department here at Allen, and attend more technology education-focused seminars and training.

Classroom Management, Communication, and Professionalism

Throughout the year, my conduct with students and staff has remained professional. All of my classroom assignments are documented on Google Classroom, behavior concerns are noted in PowerSchool, and parent contacts are maintained in ParentSquare. While my communications with parents are thoroughly documented, going forward I want to emphasize communicating with parents when I observe positive and exceptional attributes, rather than only notifying them of problems in the classroom. Every year I have extremely talented students, and it is important that not only the student knows that, but the parents as well, so they have more information to better understand what direction to guide their children in. Within the classroom, I want to stay open to feedback and continue learning from other teachers with different styles. Additionally, I am focusing on finding more ways to clearly show evidence of student growth and learning within my heavily project-based curriculum and nontraditional classroom, while continuing to streamline my administrative and scheduling commitments.

Conclusion

Reflecting on this year reminds me of why I love teaching this subject—it forces both me and my students to remain adaptable, critical thinkers. By focusing on clearer evidence of student growth, expanding my communication with families, and continuing my own professional development, I look forward to making my classroom an even more effective, accessible, and inspiring space for our future innovators.
